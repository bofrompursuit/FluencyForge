    The prompt lab

    Write a real prompt for the brief below. The checklist on the right lights up as your draft picks up each of the five elements a durable prompt needs.

    The brief

    Turn a 1,200-word blog post about our new reporting dashboard into three social posts — one for LinkedIn, two for X — aimed at marketing ops leads. Keep our voice: plain, specific, no hype.

    0 words

    Prompt checklist

    Five elements. Hit all five and your output stops being a coin flip.

    0 of 5 elements

    • ✓ Role Tell it who it's being. “You are a B2B social editor…”
    • ✓ Context The audience, the product, the voice, the source material.
    • ✓ Format Exactly what comes back: how many, how long, what shape.
    • ✓ Examples One good sample beats a paragraph of description.
    • ✓ Constraints What to avoid: no hashtags, no hype, don't invent numbers.

    All five, locked in.

    That's a prompt you can hand to a teammate and get the same output twice.

    The workflow builder

    An agent doing one big job in one shot is a coin flip. An agent doing five small ordered steps is a system. Drag these into the order you'd run them — or use the arrows.

    Competitor brief agent

    The job: produce a one-page competitor brief from three URLs. Sequence the steps.

    Why order matters

    Each step's output is the next step's context. Draft before you analyse and the agent is writing from nothing. Review before you draft and there's nothing to review.

    The two rules that catch most mistakes: gather first, and review last. Everything between those is judgement.

    On Day 3 you'll do this with your own task, and the coaches will pull it apart with you.
